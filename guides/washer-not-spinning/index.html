Washer Symptom guide

Washing machine not spinning?

A no-spin result can come from cycle selection, an unbalanced load, incomplete draining, or a door that never locked—not only a failed motor.

Stop and get help if

The washer moves violently, smells hot or electrical, leaks near wiring, or the door remains locked after the manual’s release procedure.

Start without opening the machine

Do not bypass the lid or door lock.

It prevents contact with a moving basket. Disconnect power before touching a hose, leveling feet, or any area the owner manual identifies as user-accessible.

Five checks that preserve the evidence

  1. Review the selected cycle and spin speed. Delicate, hand-wash, low-spin, or no-spin settings intentionally leave more water in fabrics.
  2. Redistribute the load. A single bulky item, overloaded drum, or bunched bedding can trigger imbalance protection and reduce or stop spin.
  3. Look for standing water. Many machines will not enter full spin until they drain. If water remains, follow the not-draining guide first.
  4. Confirm the machine is level. Follow the installation instructions; rocking or uneven feet can make recurring imbalance more likely.
  5. Record the behavior. Note the cycle, displayed code, whether the door locks, whether the basket moves at all, and any sound. That information makes a service estimate more specific.

When service becomes the next step

If balanced loads and correct settings still produce no spin, the cause can involve the lock system, drive components, motor, speed sensing, wiring, or control electronics. Those possibilities require model-specific diagnostics; replacing a part based only on the symptom is guesswork.

Use a written estimate

Ask the technician to identify the failed system, total installed price, and repair warranty. Compare that number—not a parts-only price—with a comparable machine in the repair-or-replace calculator.
