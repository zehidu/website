Washer Symptom guide

Washing machine not draining?

Start with the cycle, load, hose, and only the filter your model’s manual says you can access. Standing water does not automatically mean the pump needs replacement.

Stop and get help if

You smell burning, see damaged wiring, find water near an outlet, or would need to bypass a lock or remove a sealed panel.

Make the situation safe

Electricity and water are a dangerous combination.

Cancel the cycle, disconnect power, and close the water valves before moving a hose or opening a user-serviceable filter. Use your exact model manual; filter access and emergency-drain steps vary.

If the tub is full, prepare a shallow pan and towels. Do not tilt a full machine. If the appliance is hard-wired, inaccessible, or leaking toward electrical connections, stop and call a qualified technician.

Check from simplest to most involved

  1. Confirm the cycle state. A paused cycle, lid or door that did not latch, or a selected “rinse hold” type option can leave water behind. Follow the model manual’s cancel or drain-and-spin procedure.
  2. Reduce and rebalance the load. A tightly packed or badly unbalanced load can prevent a complete spin and leave clothes very wet, which can look like a drainage problem.
  3. Inspect the visible drain hose. Look for a kink, crushing, freezing, or an obvious blockage. Compare its height and insertion depth with the installation instructions for your model.
  4. Clean only an owner-accessible pump filter. Some front-load machines have one; others do not. Drain residual water exactly as the manual describes before loosening it.
  5. Run the manual’s drain test. If the machine hums without moving water, repeatedly stops, leaks, or shows the same code, record the sound and code for service rather than repeatedly cycling it.
Why model-specific instructions matter

Hose limits and filter locations differ. A generic measurement or panel-removal instruction can create a new leak or void a safe procedure.

What the result may point toward

A corrected load or hose route suggests an operating or installation issue. Debris in a documented user filter suggests routine maintenance. Continued standing water after those checks can involve the pump, wiring, control, pressure system, or an internal obstruction—areas better diagnosed with model-specific service information.

Repair or replace?

A drainage symptom alone is not enough to replace a washer. Get a written estimate that names the failed part and warranty. Then compare that estimate with the appliance’s age and a truly comparable replacement using our repair-or-replace calculator. Check recalls before paying for work.
