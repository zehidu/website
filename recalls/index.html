Check for a recall before paying for a repair.

A recall can change who should perform the work, which remedy is available, and whether the appliance should remain in use.

This site does not collect model or serial numbers.

The official CPSC search handles the lookup. We plan a privacy-reviewed lookup using public recall data after the first release.

Gather the right information

Photograph the manufacturer label rather than relying on memory. The useful fields are brand, model number, product type, and an approximate purchase date. Keep the serial number private unless the manufacturer or CPSC remedy process specifically requires it.

If you find a matching recall

Follow the official remedy and stop-use instructions exactly. Do not improvise a repair or continue using the product because the symptom seems minor.

Search the official database

The CPSC publishes U.S. recall announcements and provides public recall data dating back to 1973. Search both the brand and model family; formatting on the appliance label may differ from the headline.

Open the CPSC recall search ↗

Verify a possible match

  1. Compare the product type and photographs.
  2. Read the affected model and serial ranges carefully.
  3. Confirm the recall announcement is from CPSC or the manufacturer’s official domain.
  4. Use the listed contact or remedy process.
  5. Save a copy of the announcement and any case number.

What a “no results” search means

It means no matching recall was found in that search—not that the product is safe, correctly installed, or free of defects. A burning smell, shock, smoke, overheating, gas odor, or serious leak still requires immediate shutdown and qualified help.
