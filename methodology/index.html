Version 1.0 Transparent by design

How the calculator reaches a result.

The tool is a planning aid, not a diagnosis. Every threshold is visible so a visitor can disagree intelligently instead of trusting a mysterious score.

Current scope

U.S. washing machines and dishwashers. The first version compares age, a complete repair estimate, and a comparable replacement price.

The three decision inputs

  • Age: whole or partial years since the appliance entered service.
  • Repair estimate: labor, parts, diagnostic fee, tax, and any other amount required to restore operation.
  • Replacement price: a comparable appliance plus required delivery, installation, connection parts, haul-away, and tax when known.

The interface asks which appliance is being compared because the planning-life assumption differs. Version 1.0 uses 11 years for a washing machine and 10 years for a dishwasher. These are editable model assumptions in the code—not manufacturer guarantees or claims that every unit should last that long.

The version 1.0 rules

Repair result

Repair cost is no more than 40% of comparable replacement cost, and the appliance has used no more than 75% of its planning life.

Replacement result

Repair cost is at least 60% of comparable replacement cost, or the appliance has reached its planning life.

Compare result

Everything between those boundaries. The visitor should weigh the written repair warranty, repeat-failure history, delivery costs, features, and one more estimate.

The displayed percentages are calculated as repair estimate ÷ replacement price and appliance age ÷ planning life. Estimated years left is planning life minus current age, never below zero.

What overrides the cost result

An active safety recall, burning smell, electrical damage, repeated leaking, unavailable parts, a failed structural component, or a qualified technician’s safety finding can outweigh a low repair ratio. Conversely, unusual installation expense or an unusually long repair warranty can make repair more attractive than the simple score suggests.

Version 1.0 does not yet calculate energy savings. That requires a matched product record and local energy assumptions; pretending otherwise would make the result look more precise than it is.

What enters analytics

Raw age, repair cost, and replacement price are not analytics fields. A completed calculation emits only the appliance category, the result, broad repair-ratio and life-used ranges, and calculator version. This is enough to learn where the model is useful without building a financial profile of a visitor.

How updates will be governed

  1. Every logic change receives a new calculator version.
  2. Old and new result distributions remain separable in stored analytics.
  3. User outcome reports will be collected only with explicit consent.
  4. Thresholds will not change merely to increase clicks, ad views, or replacement referrals.
  5. Material changes will be dated on this page before publication.
